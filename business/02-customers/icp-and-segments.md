# Ideal Customer Profile & Segments

> Owner: _TBD_ · Status: `DRAFT` · Last reviewed: 2026-07-07

Who Granville is for. The customer is an **operating business** that consumes the regulated
financial services Granville provides — not a licensed money mover.

**Two layers — keep them straight:**

- **Public surface (what the market sees):** Granville addresses **four segments — agencies,
  influencers, marketplaces, ecommerce.** All four move money cross-border and need their books
  to tie out. This is the website/marketing framing.
- **Strategic wedge (where BD actually spends effort):** **Canada-based agencies paying foreign
  "payroll"** — recurring cross-border payouts to overseas contractors/team. This is the
  beachhead we land first, then expand across the other three public segments.

Everything below is written to the **wedge** first. Don't let the broad public four dilute the
focused motion — market to four, *sell* to one.

---

## The one-sentence ICP

> A Canadian agency or startup that pays a distributed team of foreign contractors every month,
> across multiple currencies — and is stuck reconciling those payouts, the FX, and the fees
> into books that never quite tie out, using a patchwork of a bank, Wise/PayPal, and a
> spreadsheet.

## Why this beachhead

- **Sharp, recurring, unavoidable pain.** Foreign contractor payouts happen every pay cycle —
  the reconciliation and FX mess repeats monthly and grows with headcount.
- **Granville's exact shape.** Recurring cross-border payouts + multi-currency + immutable
  booking of every payment is precisely "merged money and books." The [product portal](../00-overview/product-primer.md)
  already speaks Payments / Wallets / Balances / FX.
- **Reachable from the founder's network.** Toronto agency/startup ecosystem; warm intros.
- **Canada-first fits the regulatory path** — cleanest route for Granville to provide the
  regulated services (founder's home jurisdiction). See [regulatory-context](../01-market/regulatory-context.md).
- **They don't want to be a fintech.** An agency wants to pay its designer in Manila and have
  it booked correctly — not build treasury infrastructure.

> **Language note:** these are almost always **contractor payouts**, not formal employment
> payroll. Say "pay your global team / contractors," not "run payroll" — real payroll carries
> employment-tax and jurisdiction obligations Granville is **not** taking on. This is a hard
> line; confirm scope with counsel ([regulatory-context](../01-market/regulatory-context.md)).

## Qualifying attributes (what makes a good fit)

| Dimension | Good fit | Poor fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Payout pattern** | Recurring monthly payouts to foreign contractors | One-off or purely domestic payments |
| **Currencies** | Pays in 2+ foreign currencies | Single currency, no FX |
| **Team shape** | Distributed / remote-first agency or startup | Fully local team on domestic payroll |
| **Books pain** | Reconciling payouts + FX + fees is manual and late | Trivial finances; accountant handles it invisibly |
| **Size** | ~5–50 foreign contractors; enough volume to hurt | 1 contractor, or enterprise with a treasury team |
| **Appetite** | Wants the payout+books problem *gone* | Wants a toolkit to build their own solution |
| **Trust** | Will run real payouts through a young platform w/ references | Needs a decade-old incumbent |

## Trigger events (when a fit becomes a buyer)

- Headcount of foreign contractors just crossed the point where spreadsheets break.
- A painful, late month-end close driven by untangling Wise/PayPal payouts + FX.
- FX fees/spread on contractor payouts became visible enough to hurt.
- An investor/audit ask exposed that the books don't tie out to what was actually paid.
- Expanding the team into a new country/currency and dreading the added reconciliation.

## The alternative they're using today (what we replace)

A bank + **Wise / PayPal / Payoneer / Deel-style tool** for the payouts + **QuickBooks/Xero** +
**a spreadsheet** to reconcile it all. Nobody owns the seam between them; the founder or an ops
person eats the pain each cycle. (See [competitive-analysis](../01-market/competitive-analysis.md).)

## Firmographics (beachhead)

- **Geography:** Canada-first (HQ in Canada; contractors anywhere).
- **Type:** agencies (marketing, creative, dev/software) and startups, remote/distributed.
- **Size band:** roughly 5–50 foreign contractors; _revenue band TBD from interviews._
- **Money-movement profile:** recurring monthly cross-border payouts, 2+ currencies.

## The four public segments (expansion order)

These are the publicly marketed segments. The wedge is #1 (agencies w/ foreign payroll); the
rest are the expansion path — served publicly, but **not** where BD spends its effort yet.

| # | Public segment | Shared pain | Wedge into it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agencies** (beachhead) | Pay foreign contractors monthly; FX + reconciliation mess | Foreign "payroll" — recurring cross-border payouts |
| 2 | Influencers / creators | Cross-border brand payments in + payouts; messy books | Getting paid & paying collaborators across currencies |
| 3 | Marketplaces | Pay out many parties, account for each cleanly | Mass payouts that self-post to the ledger |
| 4 | Ecommerce | Multi-currency sales, supplier payouts, FX | Money in/out + books in one system |

> Do **not** chase segments 2–4 in the sales motion until the agency wedge has produced live
> references. Marketing can speak to all four; BD lands one.

## Anti-ICP (who to politely decline)

- Businesses wanting **formal employment payroll** across jurisdictions (tax/employment scope
  Granville isn't taking — refer out; revisit only with counsel).
- Fully domestic teams with no FX (no pain).
- Enterprises with mature treasury/ERP (long sale, not our wedge yet).
- Anyone wanting a toolkit to build their own fintech (→ BaaS).
- Use that pushes Granville into uncleared regulatory scope
  ([regulatory-context](../01-market/regulatory-context.md)).

## From ICP to person

In an agency/startup this is usually the **founder/CEO or ops/finance lead** who personally
runs the monthly payout. See [personas.md](personas.md).
