# Go-To-Market Strategy

> Owner: _TBD_ · Status: `DRAFT` · Last reviewed: 2026-07-07

How Granville actually reaches operating businesses and turns them into paying, referenceable
customers. Grounded in where the product is today (pre-first-go-live).

---

## The motion (stage-appropriate)

**Founder-led, credibility-first, design-partner-driven.** We are pre-revenue and pre-logo,
so the motion is *not* scaled outbound — it is a small number of high-trust, hands-on
relationships that produce reference customers and a repeatable story.

Sequence:

1. **Land design partners** — 3–5 operating businesses in one beachhead segment who feel the
   cross-border payout pain acutely and will co-build.
2. **Prove the moment** — get one real money flow live, posting to the ledger, tying out.
3. **Turn proof into narrative** — references, a case study, a repeatable demo.
4. **Then** scale a repeatable motion (content, partnerships, light outbound).

Do not skip to step 4. The whole company de-risks at step 2 (first go-live).

## Why founder-led fits here

- The founder is a payments/fintech lawyer — the single most credible person to explain "we
  provide the regulated services, here's how it's safe" to a nervous business buyer.
- Regulated-money trust is earned in conversations, not ads.
- Early product needs tight feedback loops that only founder-led selling gives.

## Beachhead focus (DECIDED) — market to four, sell to one

- **Public surface:** marketing speaks to **four segments — agencies, influencers,
  marketplaces, ecommerce** (all move money across borders and need a clean record of it).
- **Sales motion:** BD spends its effort on **one wedge — Canada-based agencies paying foreign
  "payroll"** (recurring cross-border contractor payouts). Sharpest pain, best product fit,
  warmest network, cleanest regulated-services path (Canada-first).

Why this split works: the broad four keep the top of funnel and the website story wide, while
the focused wedge keeps the founder-led motion efficient and the references specific. **Do not
let inbound from the other three segments pull the sales motion off the agency wedge** until the
wedge has produced live references — then expand deliberately, one segment at a time.

Full definition: [ICP](../02-customers/icp-and-segments.md). Scope line: contractor payouts,
**not** employment payroll ([regulatory-context](../01-market/regulatory-context.md)).

## The GTM funnel (define the stages we'll actually track)

| Stage | Definition | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Fit | Matches ICP | Qualified via trigger event |
| Conversation | First founder call | Pain confirmed, interest to explore |
| Design partner | Agreed to co-build/pilot | One real flow scoped |
| Live | Real money on Granville | Payment posts to ledger, ties out |
| Reference | Willing to be named / refer | Case study or intro given |
| Expansion | More volume/flows | Usage growth |

Track live deals against these stages in [pipeline.md](../06-plan/pipeline.md).

## What has to be true for GTM to work (dependencies on product)

- **Production go-live on Rapyd** — cannot get a customer "Live" without a production rail, and
  the rail is now Rapyd (Airwallex dropped on cost). Gated on the Rapyd rev-share partnership +
  integration. **Top blocker.**
- **Granville MVP** (portal, ops approvals, reporting) — needed for a business to actually run
  daily on it, not just demo.
- **Clear regulatory model** — sales cannot promise "we handle the regulated part" until the
  model is confirmed. See [regulatory-context.md](../01-market/regulatory-context.md).

BD's job right now is to line up design partners so that the *day* the Rapyd rail goes live,
there is a real customer flow ready to switch on.

## What we are explicitly NOT doing yet

- No paid acquisition / performance marketing.
- No sales hires.
- No multi-segment spray.
- No enterprise RFPs.

Focus is the strategy. One segment, one geography, a handful of design partners, one go-live.
